The methodologyFORGE — Intent-Driven Development

Most failed AI-assisted development isn't a model-capability problem — it's an intent-clarity problem. FORGE makes intent expression a first-class engineering activity, with phases, deliverables, and quality gates. You don't earn the right to generate code until you can describe what done looks like.

F

Focus

Capture intent: what, why, and success in testable terms.

O

Orchestrate

Decompose intent into work AI and humans can carry.

R

Refine

Make it verifiable: acceptance criteria, given-when-then.

G

Generate

Now the code — by agent, pair, or hand — traced to intent.

E

Evaluate

Prove outcomes match intent, with evidence on record.

Intent

The precise articulation of what you're building and why it matters — sharp enough for an agent with no intuition, only instructions.

Outcome

Intent without a measurable outcome is a wish. Every line of code traces to an outcome; every outcome traces to an intent.

Accountability

The verifiable chain from intent through outcome to evidence — what makes autonomous development trustworthy.

Plainly statedQuestions, answered

What is Intent-Driven Development?
Intent-Driven Development (IDD) is a software-development philosophy for the AI era, created by Scott Feltham. Its founding axiom: the quality of AI output is determined by the quality of the intent you express. IDD rests on three pillars — Intent, Outcome, and Accountability — and makes intent expression a first-class engineering activity with its own phases, deliverables, and quality gates.
What is the FORGE Framework?
FORGE is the delivery methodology that implements IDD, in five phases: Focus (capture intent), Orchestrate (decompose it), Refine (make it verifiable), Generate (produce code traced to intent), and Evaluate (prove outcomes match intent, with evidence). It works with any AI tool, language, or team structure.
Who is Scott Feltham?
An engineering leader with 25 years across defense systems, government digital services, SaaS, and AI platforms — VP of Engineering, Head of Engineering, and currently Chief Technology Officer. Creator of FORGE and Intent-Driven Development, and author of the forthcoming book of the same name.
Can one engineer really run a production platform through AI?
Yes — with the right discipline. As CTO, Scott operates a multi-product production AI platform alone: 1,780+ commits and 215+ tagged releases across 20 repositories in four months, 11 new services shipped, while cutting AI cost per unit 3.3× at doubled volume. The method is the point: agentic systems carry execution; the engineer's job is precise intent and rigorous verification.
